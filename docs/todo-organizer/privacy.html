2. Principle: no data collection

ToDo Organizer does not process any personal data on the developer's servers. The developer does not operate a server for the app. There are no user accounts, no sign-up, no analytics or tracking services, no advertising and no sharing of data with third parties.

3. What is stored on the device

Everything you enter in the app stays on your device only. The app stores:

  • your tasks with title, category, priority, due date and time, reminder, repeat, subtasks, note and, where applicable, a subject and an email address you assigned a task to
  • photos you add to a task (reduced in size, in the app's IndexedDB storage)
  • categories, templates, habits with the days ticked off, weekly plan or timetable (entries, rooms, times) and shopping lists with recently bought items as suggestions
  • your settings, such as visible features, appearance (light/dark), language, state or region (for public and school holidays), A/B weeks, week days and reminder time
  • cancellations, covers and duties you enter for individual lessons (with class, subject, room and note), regular break duties, notes on individual lessons and reminders “for the next lesson”
  • where applicable, the addresses of your own calendar subscriptions (ICS, up to eight, with a name and colour of your choice) and the events last loaded from them
  • whether the trial has started (with date) and whether the full version is unlocked

The data is kept in the app's protected storage (localStorage and IndexedDB). It is never transferred to the developer. Uninstalling the app removes it completely. In the app, all data can be deleted under “Settings → Tidy up”.

Please note: if you create tasks containing names or email addresses of other people, the app stores these details too – again only on your device.

4. Reminders

Reminders are scheduled with your device's operating system (local notifications). For this, the system asks for your permission once. No push service and no server is used; the title and category of due tasks only appear in the notification on your device. If you wish, the app icon shows the number of due tasks; this, too, is calculated on the device only.

Device calendar

If you tap “Connect device calendar” in Settings, iOS asks for your permission. The app then reads the events of the period shown from the calendars on your device in order to display them in the week view. The events are only used for display, are not permanently stored by the app and are not transferred. With “Add to calendar”, the app creates an event for a task at your request. The app does not read or change entries in the Reminders app. You can revoke the permission at any time in the iOS settings.

Widget

If you add the “Today” widget to your home screen, the app places a short summary of the next three days (titles and times of tasks, lessons and events) in a protected storage area that only the app and its widget can read. The data stays on the device.

Camera and photos

If you add a photo to a task, the app opens the iOS camera or photo picker. The app only receives the one photo you take or select, reduces its size and stores it on the device. Photos are not transferred; a backup file you export yourself contains them.

Public and school holidays

The app calculates public holidays itself. School holidays for Germany, Austria and Switzerland are included in the app (source: OpenHolidays API, licence ODbL). The app does not fetch anything from the internet for this; the selected state or region stays on your device.

Automatic backup

In the iPhone and iPad app, as long as the setting is turned on, the app saves a backup file once a week in its own Documents folder (visible in the Files app under “On My iPhone/iPad → ToDo Organizer”). The file stays on the device; it is part of the iOS device backup if you use it. The app does not send it anywhere.

5. Backup, export and sharing

A backup (JSON file, optionally encrypted with a password) and the export as a calendar file (.ics) are created on the device only and passed on via your device's share function. Where the file goes afterwards (e.g. to the Files app, to iCloud Drive or by email) is entirely up to you. Encryption is done on the device with AES-GCM; the password is not stored. When importing, the app only reads the one file you select.

“Share list” and the email functions open the share menu or the email app set up on the device. Sending happens there and only when you trigger it.

6. Internet connections

The app only connects to the internet by itself in these cases:

  • Calendar subscriptions: if you enter the address of a calendar (ICS) in Settings, the app fetches exactly this address to show the events – when adding it, at your request and, when the app is opened, at most every six hours. As with any request, the operator of this calendar (e.g. Google) technically learns your IP address and the time. Without an address entered, this never happens.
  • iCloud sync (iPhone/iPad, only if turned on): the app's entries (without photos and without loaded subscription events) are stored in the user's iCloud account at Apple so that all of the user's devices have the same data. Transfer and storage are handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; the developer of the app has no access to them. It can be turned off in the app's settings; the data in iCloud can be deleted in the iOS settings under iCloud → Manage Storage.
  • App Store: the trial and the full version are handled by Apple (see section 7).

The optional “Google Calendar link” opens a pre-filled Google page in the browser at your request. The app itself does not access a Google account. The app contains no external analytics libraries, no fonts from third-party servers and no embedded content.

7. Trial and in-app purchase

The trial and the full version are handled entirely via Apple's App Store. The developer of this app does not receive any payment data. The app only learns from the App Store whether and since when the trial is running and whether the full version is unlocked for your account, and remembers this on the device so that it also works offline. Apple's privacy policy applies.

8. Download via an app store

When you download the app from an app store, its operator processes data independently (such as account, device and time of download). The developer of this app has no influence on this. The privacy policy of the respective operator applies.

9. Your rights

You have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection under Art. 15 to 21 GDPR, as well as the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. As the developer does not store any personal data, requests for access or erasure addressed to the developer will generally come to nothing. You can delete all data yourself in the app at any time.
